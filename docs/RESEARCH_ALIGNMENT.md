# Research alignment and verified boundaries

This is a development mapping to the supplied Chapters 1–3, especially Sections 1.3–1.6, 3.4–3.8 and Appendices A–D. It implements rehearsal functions without claiming that the proposed research procedures or external approvals have already occurred.

## Decisions the software cannot resolve

- Chapter 1 retains significance-based questions/hypotheses, whereas Chapter 3 explicitly adopts a two-class exploratory comparison. Reconcile that with the supervisor before O1. The software does not generate treatment p-values or claim a replicated causal estimate.
- Section 2.8 appears twice in Chapter 2. Review the final chapter structure and cross-references before defence.
- Section 3.4.2 reports that intended functions are working. Replace that assertion with a version-specific verification record where a feature has not yet been demonstrated on school devices. Audio, remote authentication and central synchronisation are not present in this release.
- The first unit is a content-development starting point. It is not an already approved four-week research teaching set. The 32 school-life candidates and their sense choices need curriculum, EVP, expert and pilot review; no participant has been tested here.
- Personal written messages and discussion can support teaching, but spontaneous writing and speaking are not the primary outcomes in the supplied design. This release prioritises the specified form–meaning construct.
- The original post-quest 1/3/7/14-day continuing review proposal does not apply after S8 in this study. Distributed review occurs inside the intervention; the follow-up interval is protected.

## Data interpretation

Recognition is supported practice. A shown card is an acknowledgement, not proof of learning. Correct practice is not primary-test success. Meaning responses outside the draft accepted list are pending human review. Unobserved minutes remain null; partial sessions remain partial. No event, completion badge, observer rating or time measure is labelled as vocabulary mastery, attention or increased motivation.

One research browser run belongs to one coded learner. The first release is a local rehearsal with behavioural controls. It is not sufficient by itself for secure multi-device classroom deployment or protection against a user reading downloaded JavaScript/data. A supervised laboratory pilot is a prerequisite to a main-study release.
